Who is allowed to see your files? Who is allowed to change them? Especially in teams and collaborative scenarios it's extremely important to protect folders and files from unauthorized access.

That's what *access rights* or *permissions* are about. 
Companies pay huge salaries for sys-admins to manage these access-rights with scripts and complicated tools to keep things save and proper.

LINUX is well known for it's very good and strong access policies which made it the first choice to build up servers and huge clusters of computers. Let's face it. The whole internet is built on LINUX. By the way. Did you remark that you're working on a LINUX-Machine here?

Let's keep things short and simple here. Just imagine you want to give your financial department the permission to read and write into your /finance/-folders. It's just ONE Entry in your /finance/-template-folder and you're done. From now on, your finance department will only see the folders and files it is allowed to. You want to let them read the /infos/ too? Easy going. Can you imagine how this might ease up things significantly.

However. The most modern and flexible way to manage access-rights is by means of so called *Acess Control Lists*(ACLs). These are in principle lists which determine for each folder or file who may read them, write them, execute them, copy them, create new files, ... things like that and of course WHO is allowed to change these lists.
